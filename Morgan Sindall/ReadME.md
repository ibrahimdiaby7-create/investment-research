I analysed Morgan Sindall and developed an M&A thesis around expanding its Infrastructure division.

Rationale: Energy infrastructure is likely to remain a major investment theme over the coming years, supported by grid expansion, decarbonisation, renewables, nuclear investment and rising electricity demand from AI and data centres. This makes Morgan Sindall’s Infrastructure division a logical area for further strategic investment. Management has also indicated openness to selective M&A, while prioritising lower-risk projects, long-term public-sector relationships and exposure to energy, decarbonisation and nuclear.

Recommended acquisition: Powersystems UK

Powersystems is a specialist electrical engineering contractor focused on high-voltage infrastructure, substations and grid connections. In 2024, the company generated approximately £64m of revenue at an ~12% EBIT margin.

In my base-case scenario, I assumed £8m of normalised EBITDA and an 8.0x EV/EBITDA entry multiple, implying a £64m enterprise value and approximately £76.8m of total transaction uses. I assumed an all-cash transaction given Morgan Sindall’s substantial net cash position.

The strategic rationale is based on two sources of value creation: bringing currently outsourced electrical work in-house and enabling Morgan Sindall to compete for additional specialist electrical packages. Under my base-case synergy assumptions, the synergy-adjusted acquisition multiple falls to approximately 3.4x EBITDA.
